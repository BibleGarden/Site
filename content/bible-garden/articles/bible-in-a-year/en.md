To read the Bible in a year, aim for about three or four chapters a day: the 66 books in this plan contain 1,189 chapters. Listening takes about 11–14 minutes a day with the English recordings in Bible Garden, before any pauses. The calendar below gives you a daily reading from the Old Testament and either the New Testament or Psalms, or you can read straight through. Choose a pace that leaves time to pay attention.

## Your 365-day Bible reading calendar
<!-- screen: classic-reading -->

Choose either reading order above the calendar:

- **Parallel** has two readings each day. The first follows the Old Testament from Genesis to Malachi, leaving out Psalms. The second follows the New Testament from Matthew to Revelation on days 1–260, then Psalms on days 261–365.
- **Straight through** follows the books in order from Genesis to Revelation.

Both plans spread the reading across 365 days using the length of Bob Souer's English BSB recording: a day with longer chapters has fewer of them. They cover the 66 books found in Protestant Bibles; Catholic and Orthodox editions include additional books that are outside this calendar. Psalms use the numbering familiar in English Bibles.

Choose a start date to put dates beside the readings. You can begin any day of the year. Use the print button to expand the months and print a copy you can mark by hand.

## Three ways to read the Bible in a year

The three common approaches change the order, not the amount: each takes you through the same 1,189 chapters once.

| Plan | Reading order | A good fit for | What to expect | In this calendar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight through** | Genesis to Revelation in book order | Readers who want to follow the Bible as printed | Leviticus begins on day 30; you reach Matthew on day 276 | **Straight through** above |
| **Old and New Testaments in parallel** | Old Testament and New Testament through day 260; Old Testament and Psalms from day 261 | Most readers, including beginners trying the whole Bible | You follow two threads at once | **Parallel** above |
| **Chronological** | Passages arranged by the events they describe, such as Job near Genesis and David's psalms near his story | Readers who already know the books and want to connect their history | The sequence requires frequent jumps and varies between editors | [Chronological 365-day plan](/articles/chronological-bible-reading-plan/) |

We suggest **Parallel** for a first full read-through. Gospels and letters sit alongside the harder Old Testament passages, giving you another way into the day's reading. Robert Murray M'Cheyne's well-known plan also combines different parts of Scripture, though it reads the New Testament and Psalms twice in a year.

If you have never read a whole book of the Bible, a year can feel like a large first commitment. Try our [seven-day plan for the Gospel of John](/articles/how-to-start-reading-the-bible/) first, then return when a daily rhythm feels familiar.

## How long does it take each day?
<!-- screen: reader-picker -->

The average is 3.3 chapters a day. These are the lengths of complete, 66-book English recordings available in Bible Garden, divided by 365 days:

| Translation and narrator | Full recording | Average per day |
|---|---:|---:|
| Berean Standard Bible (BSB), Bob Souer | 69.2 hours | 11.4 minutes |
| Berean Standard Bible (BSB), David | 78.9 hours | 13.0 minutes |
| World English Bible, US edition (WEBUS), Winfred Henson | 84.4 hours | 13.9 minutes |
| World English Bible, British edition (WEBBE), eBible.org, narrator uncredited | 66.2 hours | 10.9 minutes |

Winfred Henson's WEBUS recording is in the public domain. The calendar days were balanced against Bob Souer's BSB audio, so an individual day may take more or less time with another narrator. These averages count playback only; leave extra time for pauses and reflection.

## Slow down and pay attention
<!-- screen: settings-pause -->

A one-year goal can turn reading into a race to check off chapters. If a verse holds your attention, read it again. Taking an extra week is better than rushing through passages you want to understand.

Ways to make room for that attention:

- **Pause the audio.** In [Bible Garden](/), you can add a pause after each verse, paragraph, or section, for a few seconds or until you resume playback.
- **Ask one question.** What does this passage say about God? What does it reveal about people? What might you carry into today?
- **Reflect with Lampada.** In our app [Lampada](https://lampada.app/), set a topic—for example, reflect on today's chapters or a passage that stayed with you. The app asks guiding questions, suggests related Scripture passages, and saves your thoughts in a private journal.

## How to listen to the Bible in a year
<!-- screen: lock-screen-player -->

Audio makes it easier to keep reading on a busy day: listen on a walk, during a commute, or while doing chores. Bible Garden is a free app for iPhone and iPad:

- **Chapters play in sequence.** The next chapter of the same book starts automatically; open the day's second reading separately. You can control playback from the lock screen.
- **Choose a narrator.** BSB has recordings by Bob Souer and David. WEBUS is read by Winfred Henson; the WEBBE recording from eBible.org has no named narrator.
- **Follow your progress.** A chapter is marked complete when you have listened to 90% of its audio (the default threshold) or read its text to the end. The app shows progress by book and across the whole Bible.

The reading plan itself is on this page: Bible Garden does not have built-in reading plans. Use the calendar to choose today's chapters and the app to read, listen, and track what you finish.

## Read the Bible in two languages
<!-- screen: multi-reading -->

You can also pair English with Russian or Ukrainian. In Multi Reading, a verse plays in one language and then in the other; on screen, the matching verse appears beneath it. That can help a bilingual family read together or give a language learner a familiar passage to compare. The screen here shows John 1 in Russian (the Kulakov translation, BTI) and English (BSB, Bob Souer).

An English and Russian pairing takes about 28–31 minutes of audio a day with BSB and a Russian recording by Ilya Prudovsky or Nikita Semyonov-Prozorovsky—about half an hour before pauses. If you need a shorter session, use Multi Reading for just one of the day's two readings, perhaps the New Testament.

## How to keep going through the year
<!-- screen: progress -->

Leviticus and Numbers can slow a first-time reader down; so can missing a week and feeling you must catch up. A few habits help:

- **Pick a regular moment.** Read with your morning coffee, on the train, or before bed.
- **Missed a day? Resume where you stopped.** Set the start date to today minus the number of days you have completed, so your next unread day falls on today and the remaining readings get new dates. You may finish later than planned, and that is fine.
- **Switch between text and audio.** Read at home and listen when you are out.
- **Notice what you have finished.** Marks on a printed calendar or the app's progress percentage show how far you have come.
- **Read with someone.** A friend or church group can follow the same plan and talk through difficult passages together.

Reading Scripture regularly has a long history. Acts describes the people of Berea this way:

> Now the Bereans were more noble-minded than the Thessalonians, for they received the message with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see if these teachings were true.
>
> — Acts 17:11, BSB

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What is the 365 day plan for reading the Bible?

It divides all 1,189 chapters in the 66-book calendar into daily readings. Choose **Parallel** for Old Testament plus New Testament through day 260, then Old Testament plus Psalms; choose **Straight through** for Genesis to Revelation. Set any start date in the calendar above and print it if you like.

### Can the Bible be read in a year?

Yes. The calendar averages 3.3 chapters a day. It is a guide, not a deadline: if you need longer with a passage or miss a day, continue from where you stopped. Beginners may find it easier to start with [one week in John](/articles/how-to-start-reading-the-bible/).

### How long does it take to read the Bible?

Listening to all 66 books takes 69.2 hours with Bob Souer's BSB, 78.9 hours with David's BSB, 84.4 hours with Winfred Henson's WEBUS, or 66.2 hours with the uncredited WEBBE narrator. Over a year that is about 10.9–13.9 minutes per day before pauses. Silent reading time varies with your pace.

### Can I listen to the Bible in a year?

Yes. Use the same daily calendar and listen in Bible Garden on iPhone or iPad. The next chapter in the same book plays automatically; open the day's second reading separately. The app tracks completed chapters, while the reading plan stays on this page.
