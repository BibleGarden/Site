The Gospel of the day is the passage selected for a church service on a particular date. There is no single reading shared by all churches: Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox communities follow different lectionaries, or schedules of Scripture readings. For today’s passage, use your church’s calendar; below are places to look, an explanation of the cycles, and a chapter-a-day plan for reading all four Gospels in 89 days.

## Where to find today’s Gospel reading

For **Catholic daily Mass readings in the United States**, start with the [USCCB daily readings](https://bible.usccb.org/). For churches using the **Revised Common Lectionary**, the [Vanderbilt lectionary site](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/faq/) explains the schedule and its daily readings. For **Orthodox readings**, check [Orthocal](https://orthocal.info/) or the [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese’s Online Chapel](https://www.goarch.org/chapel/).

Choose the calendar your parish follows. Even within Orthodoxy, the same civil date can have different readings under the old and new calendars. A feast or a local observance can also change the selection, so your parish’s schedule is the best guide if you are preparing for a service.

## How the reading cycles work

A lectionary brings Scripture into the rhythm of the church year. It is different from a plan that takes you through a book from beginning to end.

| Tradition | Main cycle | Where the Gospels fit |
|---|---|---|
| Roman Catholic | Sundays: three years, A, B, and C. Weekday first readings: two years, I and II. | Sunday readings emphasize Matthew in A, Mark in B, and Luke in C. John appears during Easter and at other points, including John 6 in Year B. The weekday Gospel cycle repeats annually. |
| Revised Common Lectionary | Three-year Sunday cycle, A, B, and C, used by many mainline Protestant churches. | Typically an Old Testament reading, a psalm, an Epistle, and a Gospel. Daily readings accompany the Sunday cycle. |
| Byzantine Orthodox | Annual cycle beginning at Pascha, or Easter. | John and Acts after Pascha; Matthew after Pentecost, with Mark on later weekdays; Luke in the fall; Mark again on winter weekdays and much of Lent’s weekends. |

Sources: the Catholic [Lectionary for Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordo\_Lectionum\_Missae), [Vanderbilt’s RCL FAQ](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/faq/), and the Orthodox [annual reading guide](https://azbyka.ru/days/p-ukazatel-evangelskih-i-apostolskih-chtenij-na-kazhdyj-den-goda) (in Russian).

Many Orthodox churches adjust the start of Luke’s readings to the Monday after the Sunday following the feast of the Exaltation of the Cross. This is called the **Lukan jump**. The calendar and this adjustment both matter: “new calendar” alone does not identify a reading schedule.

On ordinary Orthodox Lenten weekdays, there is usually no full Divine Liturgy, and the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts has no ordinary Epistle or Gospel reading. Isaiah, Genesis, and Proverbs are read instead. Feasts and the first three days of Holy Week have exceptions. Revelation is not read at the Divine Liturgy.

## Why daily passages do not replace a whole Gospel

A short passage can stay with you through the day. But a lectionary follows seasons and feasts, rather than the narrative order of a book. You may hear a passage about the Resurrection, then return to an earlier part of Jesus’ ministry.

Reading a Gospel straight through lets you follow its own story: how Jesus meets people, teaches, travels toward Jerusalem, and faces his death and Resurrection. You can keep your church’s daily reading and read a chapter alongside it.

## Read all four Gospels in 89 days

Matthew has 28 chapters, Mark 16, Luke 24, and John 21: **89 chapters altogether**. One chapter a day takes just under three months. Here is one order that starts with the shortest Gospel:

| Days | Gospel | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 1–16 | Mark | 1–16 |
| 17–44 | Matthew | 1–28 |
| 45–68 | Luke | 1–24 |
| 69–89 | John | 1–21 |

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### What is today’s Gospel reading?

It depends on your church’s lectionary and calendar. Use the USCCB for US Catholic Mass readings, Vanderbilt for the Revised Common Lectionary, or your Orthodox parish’s calendar and the resources above. This English article does not calculate a daily reading.

### Do Catholic weekday readings repeat every year?

The weekday Gospel cycle repeats annually. The first reading follows a two-year cycle, Year I and Year II; Sundays use the separate three-year A/B/C cycle. Feast days can have their own readings.

### Is the Revised Common Lectionary only for Sundays?

Its main three-year cycle is for Sundays and festivals. There are also daily readings associated with that cycle; Vanderbilt explains how they relate to the Sunday passages. Check which schedule your congregation uses.

### Why do Orthodox calendars sometimes show different passages?

Fixed feasts may follow different calendars, and churches may apply different adjustments to the reading cycle, including the Lukan jump. Saints’ commemorations and local observances can also affect the readings.

### Can I listen offline in Bible Garden?

Audio playback requires an internet connection. The app does not currently let you download recordings for offline listening.

### Can I finish all four Gospels in 90 days?

Yes. They contain 89 chapters, so one chapter a day takes 89 days. Start with Mark for the shortest book, or with our [introductory week in John](/articles/how-to-start-reading-the-bible/) if you are new to the Bible.
