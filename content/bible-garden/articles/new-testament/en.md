## The order of the New Testament books

English Bibles arrange the New Testament like this:

1. **Gospels and Acts**: Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts.
2. **Paul's letters**: Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon. Hebrews follows them; its author isn't named, and some lists count it with Paul's letters.
3. **General letters**: James, 1 and 2 Peter, 1, 2, and 3 John, Jude.
4. **Revelation**.

Russian Bibles put the general letters right after Acts, ahead of Paul. The books themselves are the same. Bible Garden lists the books in that Russian order in every language, under the headings "Gospels & Acts," "General Epistles," "Paul's Epistles," and "Prophetic," so look for James right after Acts.

Neither order is chronological. Most scholars date 1 Thessalonians to about AD 49–51, making it the earliest surviving book of the New Testament ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Epistle_to_the_Thessalonians)), and see Mark, written around AD 70, as the earliest Gospel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark)). The Gospels come first not because they were written first, but because they tell the story everything else depends on: the life of Jesus.

## What order to read the New Testament in for the first time

Reading straight through from Matthew to Revelation is a perfectly good plan. But on a first read, many people find it easier when narrative alternates with the denser letters. Here is one route:

1. **Mark**: the shortest Gospel, fast-moving, one event after another.
2. **Luke, then Acts right after it.** They are two volumes of one work, written to the same man. Luke says he set out "to write an orderly account for you, most excellent Theophilus" (Luke 1:3), and Acts opens: "In my first book, O Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began to do and to teach" (Acts 1:1; both quotes from the Berean Standard Bible).
3. **Paul's letters.** After Acts, you know who Paul is, where he traveled, and whom he's writing to.
4. **Matthew and John**: the same story from other angles. Matthew gathers much of Jesus' teaching; John records His long conversations.
5. **Hebrews and the general letters.**
6. **Revelation** last: a book of visions and symbols is easier to read once you know the rest of the New Testament.

Well-known plans follow a similar idea. The Navigators' 5x5x5 New Testament plan (one chapter a day, five days a week) starts with Mark and Acts, spreads the Gospels across the year, and ends with Revelation ([Navigators plan, PDF](https://www.navigators.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/navigators-5x5x5-new-testament-bible-reading-plan.pdf)).

If the Bible is completely new to you and you'd like to start small, try the [7-day plan with the Gospel of John](/articles/how-to-start-reading-the-bible/), then continue with this list.

## Listen slowly: pauses between verses
<!-- screen: settings-pause -->

The New Testament is dense: a single sentence in one of Paul's letters can be worth a long pause. To keep the audio from turning into background noise, Bible Garden can pause after every verse, paragraph, or section, either for a few seconds or until you tap Play. A 2-second pause after every verse adds about 4½ hours to the New Testament, or about 3 minutes a day on a three-chapter plan. To go back to a phrase, the previous and next verse buttons jump to the exact start of a verse.

All the English recordings are voice only, with no music, so nothing competes with the text.

Audio keeps playing in the background and with the screen off. You need an internet connection to listen: the app can't download recordings for offline use yet.

## Frequently asked questions {#faq}

### How many books and chapters are in the New Testament?

27 books and 260 chapters: four Gospels, Acts, 13 letters of Paul, Hebrews, seven general letters, and Revelation. Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Bibles share the same New Testament.

### How long does it take to listen to the New Testament?

In Bible Garden, the English recordings run about 15½ to 18½ hours: 15 h 24 m for the WEBBE, 16 h 50 m for Bob Souer's BSB, 18 h 6 m for Winfred Henson's WEB, and 18 h 38 m for David's BSB. The four Gospels alone take 7 to 8½ hours. Reading silently takes about 12 hours.

### Which Gospel should I read or listen to first?

Mark: it's the shortest, with 16 chapters and about 1½ hours of audio. If the Bible is new to you, John is another good start, with many of Jesus' own conversations.

### Can I read the New Testament in 30 days?

Yes: that's 9 chapters a day, about 32–39 minutes of English audio. In 90 days, it's three chapters a day, about 11–13 minutes.

### Does Bible Garden have a New Testament reading plan?

There are no built-in plans, but a simple plan is easy to keep on your own: three chapters a day takes 87 days at 11–13 minutes of audio, and one chapter a day takes 260 days at about 4 minutes. The app marks finished chapters automatically and, on the progress screen, offers to open your first unread chapter in each book.
