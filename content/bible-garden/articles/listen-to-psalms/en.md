## How to play a psalm
<!-- screen: classic-reading-psalm -->

1. Open Classic Reading and choose the book of Psalms.
2. Choose a chapter: in Psalms, each chapter is one psalm.
3. In the settings, choose a translation and a narrator.

The text is highlighted as the narrator reads, so you can follow along. Audio keeps playing in the background, and you can pause and resume it from the lock screen. The next psalm starts automatically; to stop after one, turn off autoplay in the settings. The app marks a psalm as read on its own.

Bible Garden is free, with no ads or in-app purchases, and its code is open source. You need an internet connection to listen, since audio cannot be downloaded in advance yet. The app runs on iPhone and iPad; listening right on this website and an Android version are in our plans. For other free apps, see "[Free audio Bible apps](/articles/free-audio-bible-apps/)."

## Why psalm numbers differ between Bibles

All the English translations in the app (BSB, WEB, and WEBBE) number the psalms by the Hebrew text, as Protestant Bibles and most modern Catholic translations do. Eastern Orthodox Bibles and Catholic liturgical texts such as the Roman Missal follow the Greek Septuagint ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms#Numbering)). The Septuagint joins Psalms 9 and 10 and Psalms 114 and 115, and splits Psalms 116 and 147. As a result, the numbers match only for Psalms 1–8 and 148–150; for most of the rest, the Greek number is one lower.

| Opening line (BSB) | BSB and WEB (Hebrew) | Septuagint numbering | Bob Souer |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The Lord is my shepherd" | 23 | 22 | 0:45 |
| "God is our refuge and strength" | 46 | 45 | 1:24 |
| "Have mercy on me, O God" | 51 | 50 | 2:07 |
| "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High" | 91 | 90 | 1:32 |
| "Blessed are those whose way is blameless" | 119 | 118 | 14:31 |
| "I lift up my eyes to the hills" | 121 | 120 | 0:45 |
| "O Lord, You have searched me and known me" | 139 | 138 | 2:28 |

In the app, the Russian Synodal and Kulakov translations and the Ukrainian New Translation (NPU) use the Greek numbering; the Ukrainian Khomenko translation uses the Hebrew one. So if a friend reading a Russian Bible mentions Psalm 50, they mean Psalm 51 in yours.

## Listen slowly: pauses after each verse
<!-- screen: settings-pause -->

The Psalms are prayers and songs, and played back to back they can easily fade into background noise. In the reading settings, you can add a pause after every verse: a few seconds, or a full stop until you tap Play. After "The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want," you then have time to repeat the line to yourself.

Pauses add time: three seconds after each verse adds about two hours to the whole book, and 18 seconds to the six verses of Psalm 23. To hear a verse again, the previous and next verse buttons jump to the exact start of a verse, and tapping Play during a pause skips it.

## Two translations, verse by verse

<div class="article-callout" markdown="1">
**Multi Reading is Bible Garden's key feature.** Each verse plays in several translations in a row, with a pause between them: for example, first the BSB read by Bob Souer, then the WEB read by Winfred Henson. On screen, each verse appears in both translations. Hearing a line twice in different words helps it sink in.
</div>

For the Psalms, pair the BSB with the WEB: both use the Hebrew numbering, and their verse counts match in all 150 psalms. Multi Reading lines up verses by their order within the same chapter number and does not convert psalm numbers. Paired with a Russian translation, it would play different psalms: BSB Psalm 23 alongside Synodal Psalm 23, which is Psalm 24 in the BSB.

With two translations, listening takes about twice as long, so Multi Reading works best for one or two psalms at a time. About 4 hours 18 minutes with Bob Souer, 4 hours 34 minutes with David, 4 hours 58 minutes with Winfred Henson, and 4 hours 2 minutes for the British Edition. An average psalm takes under two minutes; the longest, Psalm 119, takes 14 to 17 minutes.

### Why is Psalm 23 numbered 22 in some Bibles?

The English translations in Bible Garden, like Protestant Bibles and most modern Catholic ones, number the psalms by the Hebrew text, while Orthodox Bibles and Catholic liturgical texts follow the Greek Septuagint. The numbers match only for Psalms 1–8 and 148–150; for most others, the Greek number is one lower.

Not yet. Bible Garden streams audio over the internet, and downloading it in advance is not available.
